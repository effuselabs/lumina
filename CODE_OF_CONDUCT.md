# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume good faith. Disagree about the work rather than
about each other.

Lumina is a small project with a small number of contributors, so this does not
need to be long — but it does need to exist, so that nobody has to guess what
happens if something goes wrong.

## What is expected

- Take the other person's point seriously before disagreeing with it
- Give criticism about code, decisions and ideas — not about the person
- Accept that other people have different experience, tools and constraints
- When you are wrong, say so and move on; nobody keeps score here

## What is not acceptable

Harassment of any kind, including unwanted personal attention. Insults or
demeaning comments, including about someone's background, identity or ability.
Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained disruption of discussion.
Anything you would not say to the person face to face in front of people you
respect.

## Reporting

If someone is behaving this way, tell the maintainer:
[@jshields-ca](https://github.com/jshields-ca), by direct message or by opening
a private security advisory on this repository if you would rather it not be
public. Reports are taken seriously, kept confidential, and answered.

If the person you need to report is the maintainer, GitHub's own
[report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) route exists precisely
for that, and using it is entirely reasonable.

## What happens next

A warning first, in most cases, because most problems are a bad day rather than
a bad person. Repeated or serious behaviour means removal from the project's
spaces. There is no appeals committee — it is a small project — but you will be
told what happened and why.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else people
are acting on behalf of the project.
